# PT-BR Style Guide for Skyscanner

## Critical Requirements
**Tone of Voice**: Be a **Geeky Travel Pal**. Tone must be **playful, geeky, and helpful**.
- **Playful**: Use a conversational, lively rhythm. Avoid corporate jargon and "trying too hard" to be funny.
- **Geeky**: Show excitement for data/patterns. Explain complex info simply (knowledgeable friend, not a lecture).
- **Helpful**: Foundation of all copy. Prioritize clarity over wordplay.
- **Localization**: If wordplay (rhyme/pun) doesn't adapt naturally, **discard it**. Prioritize meaning and cultural fit over literal translation.
- **Formality**: Always use **"você" / "vocês"**. **Never use "tu"**.

## Linguistic Guidelines

### Punctuation & Spacing
- **Commas**: Use sparingly. Split long sentences to avoid confusion.
- **Exclamation Marks**: Use rarely. Suitable only for clear CTAs or direct dialogue. Do not use for "marketing excitement."
- **Quotes**: Use **double quotes (" ")** first; **single quotes (' ')** only for nested items. Use straight quotes, not curly.
- **Slashes**: No spaces before/after slashes between words or numbers (e.g., `dispositivo móvel/celular`, not `dispositivo móvel / celular`).
- **Semicolons**: Avoid. Use to separate full sentences only; do not use for lists of single words.
- **Currency**: Use a **non-breaking space** between currency symbol and value (e.g., `US$ 100`, not `US$100`).
- **Ampersands**: Translate as "e" (e.g., `Voos e hotéis`), unless part of a proper brand name (e.g., `Dolce & Gabbana`).

### Capitalization & Dates
- **Sentence Case**: Mandatory for titles and CTAs.
- **Dates**:
    - Full: `11 de janeiro de 2026`.
    - First of month: `1º de fevereiro`.
    - Abbreviated: `11/1/2026` (no zero-padding, no trailing zeros).
- **Colon**: Do not capitalize the word after a colon on the same line.

### Addressing Users
- **Active Voice**: Mandatory for all headlines and CTAs.
- **Inclusivity**: Use gender-neutral language. Avoid stereotypes or assumptions about age, family status, or ability.

### Currency & Units (home market: Brazil)
- **Currency — on hold, do not change**: the currency convention for pt-BR is under review. **Do not flag, convert, or rewrite the currency symbol** used in the source, and do **not** apply the global home-market currency rule to pt-BR content. Only the **formatting** rule above applies (non-breaking space, e.g. `US$ 100`).
- **Home-market measurement system**: **metric** (km, m, °C, litros, kg). Convert destination measurements to metric and show **only** the metric value. `18 milhas do centro` → **Compliance · Major** → `cerca de 29 km do centro`.
- **Exception — speed limits** (and other posted road signage at the destination): keep the **destination's unit first**, then the metric value in parentheses: `100 mph (cerca de 160 km/h)`. A speed limit shown in only one unit, or with km/h first → **Compliance · Major**.

## Technical Requirements

### URLs
- **Format**: All lowercase, no special characters (use basic Latin), no spaces.
- **Separator**: Use hyphen (`-`) for word separation.

### Placeholders (Tags/Variables)
- **Integrity**: **Do not translate, delete, or change placeholders**. Keep exact syntax.
- **Spacing**: Ensure spaces exist before/after tags to prevent words "gluing" together when populated.
- **Prepositions**: If a placeholder causes preposition issues (e.g., city name), rephrase to avoid them (e.g., `@@city@@: os preços...` instead of `em @@city@@`).

### Airport Names
- **Translation**: Never add the word "aeroporto" if it is not in the source name.
- **Example**: Fly from `@@airport-name@@` Airport -> `Parta do Aeroporto @@airport-name@@`.

## Content Type Guidelines

### UI
- Short, clear, action-focused. One idea per string. Use consistent terminology (e.g., always "Alerta de Preço").

### Marketing
- Creative, fluid, inspirational. Focus on traveler benefits.

### SEO
- Natural language, no keyword stuffing. Clear headings.

### Legal / Policies
- **Neutrality**: Precise, factual, unemotional. No jokes or idioms. Do not interpret; stick strictly to the source.

### Sustainability Content (Strict Rules)
- **Avoid Greenwashing**: Never imply a flight is "sustainable", "green", "clean", or "eco".
- **Comparatives Only**: Use relative statements (e.g., "emits 20% less carbon than...").
- **Avoid Vague Terms**: Do not use "natural", "non-toxic", or "environmentally friendly" without verifiable, standard criteria.
- **Avoid Imagery**: Do not use nature-themed metaphors that imply aviation is harmless.

## Brand & Legal Constraints

### Skyscanner Brand Name
- **Immutable**: Never translate "Skyscanner".

### Feature Naming
- **Meaning over Literal**: If an English name is misleading (e.g., "Packages" sounding like postal parcels), use the local equivalent (e.g., "pacotes de viagem"). Only keep English names if widely understood.

### "Best" & Superlatives
- **Restriction**: Do not claim "best prices" or "best deals" (unsubstantiated/competitive claim).
- **Exceptions**: Only use "Best" if there is an explicit disclaimer explaining how "best" is measured. Otherwise, use subjective terms like "epic prices" or "amazing deals".

### "Discount" & "Deals"
- **Legal Restriction**: Skyscanner never offers discounts.
- **Phrasing**:
    - Never use "Skyscanner offers a discount". Use neutral phrasing: "You are offered a discount".
    - **Never** translate "price drops" or "deals" as "desconto" (discount). This is legally incorrect.

### Anglicisms
- **Use Sparingly**: Only use if widely accepted (e.g., brand names, common tech terms). Ensure they fit the tone.

## Quick Reference: Dos and Don'ts

| Category | Do | Don't |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CTA** | `Reserve já` | `Reserve` (if longer than 1 word) |
| **Punctuation** | `Ops, parece que não funcionou` | `Ops!!!! Parece que não funcionou...` |
| **Slashes** | `móvel/celular` | `móvel / celular` |
| **Dates** | `11/1/2026` | `11/01/2026` |
| **Airport** | `Parta do Aeroporto Charles de Gaulle` | `Parta do Aeroporto Aeroporto Charles de Gaulle` |
| **Discounts** | Use "ofertas", "redução de preço" | Use "desconto" (for price drops/deals) |
| **Address** | Use "você" | Use "tu" |
| **Sustainability**| "emite 20% menos carbono" | "voo sustentável/verde" |


## SEO / Editorial LQA Addendum (Jan 2026 + MosAIQ edit patterns)
Source: Style Guide Jan 2026 (Portuguese Brazil) and client training edits. Additive — does not override Critical Requirements above.

### Register (mandatory)
- Always use **você / vocês**. Flag any **tu / tua / teu / teus / tuas** or second-person *tu* verb forms as **Compliance** (Major unless isolated typo).
- Style guide wins even if a human gold edit left some *tu* forms.

### Geo naming & articles
- Use official/common local names; keep consistent. Prefer natural Brazilian Portuguese articles where required for regions/states (e.g. **A Bahia**, **O Rio Grande do Sul**) when omission sounds ungrammatical.
- Correct place-name prepositions (e.g. **Praia de Laranjeiras**, **orla do Rio Guaíba**) when the established local form requires them.
- Do not invent new place names; only fix clear article/preposition/established-name issues.

### SEO body readability (objective Style only)
- For SEO/editorial body under an H2: if a **single paragraph packs multiple distinct ideas** and is objectively hard to scan on first reading, flag **Style** and provide a split in `updated_sentence` / `corrected_content` (separate short paragraphs with blank lines between).
- Do **not** flag synonym preference, mild repetition, or “sounds nicer” rewrites.
- Prefer clarity: commas where needed to separate clauses; avoid synonym-only lexical swaps.

### Typography
- Prefer spaced hyphen for airport name connectors when an en-dash confuses display (e.g. `Salvador - Deputado` over `Salvador–Deputado`) if it improves scanability without changing meaning.
