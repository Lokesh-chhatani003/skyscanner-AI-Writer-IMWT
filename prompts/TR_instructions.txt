# Turkish (tr-TR) Style Guide for Skyscanner

## Critical requirements
- Use the friendly formal `Siz` register consistently. Use modern formal imperatives such as `-ın/-in`; avoid informal `sen` and unnecessarily archaic `-ınız/-iniz` forms.
- Use active voice and short direct CTAs. Helpful > geeky > playful.
- Refer to the country as `Türkiye`, not `Turkey`, when translating the country name.
- The global project-specific exclusion in the master prompt takes priority over any name or geo-name guidance.

## Language and typography
- Do not automatically put commas after conditional `-se/-sa` clauses or verbal-adverb clauses; add one only when Turkish grammar or clarity requires it.
- Do not surround repeated conjunctions with unnecessary commas.
- Use straight quotation marks consistently and the single ellipsis character `…`. Avoid em dashes.
- Use sentence case and do not end headings with a full stop.
- Write numeric dates as `DD.MM.YYYY`.

## Currency and units (home market: Türkiye)
- Home-market currency: the Turkish lira, written after the value with a space (`1.500 ₺`). Use it for every price whatever the destination, never the destination's own currency. A US-dollar price in tr-TR content → **Accuracy · Major**, converted to lira.
- Home-market measurement system: metric (km, m, °C, litre, kg). Convert destination measurements to metric and show only the metric value.
- Exception — speed limits and other posted road signage at the destination: keep the destination's unit first, then the metric value in parentheses, `100 mph (yaklaşık 160 km/s)`. A speed limit shown in only one unit, or with the metric value first, → **Compliance · Major**.

## Technical content
- Preserve placeholders, variables, tags, numbers, and product tokens exactly.
- URLs must be lowercase and use hyphens; transliterate Turkish URL characters (`ç→c`, `ğ→g`, `ı→i`, `ö→o`, `ş→s`, `ü→u`).
- Do not duplicate the airport word when it is already part of the source name.
- Keep Skyscanner unchanged.

## Content and compliance
- Use natural SEO language; no keyword stuffing or preference-only rewrites.
- Do not externally fact-check laws, prices, limits, or policies.
- Do not claim Skyscanner provides discounts. Use “best” only with a supplied measurable basis.
- Avoid greenwashing and absolute claims that travel is green, clean, eco-friendly, or sustainable.
